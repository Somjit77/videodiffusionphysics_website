Intuitive physics is not unique to self-supervised encoders like V-JEPA. Video diffusion models, trained only to generate, encode it too: whether a real video is physically plausible can be read linearly from their hidden states, on par with or better than V-JEPA 2 and VideoMAE. The signal is absent from the VAE latents the models operate on and emerges inside the denoising transformer.

Abstract

Modern video diffusion models generate increasingly realistic and temporally coherent videos, motivating their use as candidate world simulators. Yet it remains unclear whether these models internally encode physical structure, or merely reproduce motion patterns seen during training. We study this question by probing video diffusion models along latent trajectories corresponding to real videos with known physical plausibility. To obtain such trajectories, we approximately invert the deterministic sampling process by integrating the learned velocity field backward from a clean video latent to noise, giving access to the model's intermediate states and attention maps. Using these recovered trajectories, we show that physical plausibility is linearly decodable from diffusion transformer states across IntPhys and InfLevel, reaching around 81.27% average accuracy and outperforming dedicated representation-learning baselines such as V-JEPA and VideoMAE. Surprisingly, this signal is absent from the VAE latent input and emerges inside the denoising transformer itself, despite the model not being trained with a self-supervised predictive objective. These findings suggest that physically meaningful representations can arise as a byproduct of generative denoising.

Method

Getting inside the model on real videos

A flow-based video model generates by integrating a learned velocity field, \(\tfrac{d\mathbf{Z}_t}{dt} = v_\theta(\mathbf{Z}_t, t)\), from noise at \(t=0\) to a clean latent at \(t=1\). A real video has no such trajectory, so the model's internal states for it are never computed. We recover one by running the sampler in reverse.

  1. 1

    Invert

    Encode the video with the model's VAE, \(\mathbf{Z}_1 = \mathcal{E}(X)\), and integrate backwards to noise. The exact inverse of an Euler step is implicit, so we evaluate the velocity at the known endpoint instead:

    \[\mathbf{Z}_{t_k} = \mathbf{Z}_{t_{k+1}} - \Delta t_k \, v_\theta(\mathbf{Z}_{t_{k+1}},\, t_{k+1})\]

    One network evaluation per step, the same cost as generation. Its deviation from the exact inverse shrinks linearly with step size, and the recovered noise regenerates the original video up to minor artifacts.

  2. 2

    Probe

    Along the 100-step trajectory, record every transformer block's output at 11 noise levels, mean-pool it over tokens, and fit one linear classifier per block and noise level:

    \[\hat{y} = \operatorname{softmax}\!\big(W_{b,t}\,\bar{\mathbf{h}}_{b,t} + \mathbf{c}_{b,t}\big)\]

    Held-out accuracy on a 60/40 split, averaged over five seeds. Each clip is classified on its own.

  3. 3

    Intervene

    During generation, corrupt one block's output at every denoising step, re-invert the result, and score it with the probe's surprise \(\psi = \text{logit}_\text{implausible} - \text{logit}_\text{plausible}\):

    \[\tilde{\mathbf{h}} = \mathbf{h} + \alpha\, \boldsymbol{\sigma}(\mathbf{h}) \odot \boldsymbol{\epsilon}, \qquad \Delta_b = \bar{\psi}(V_b) - \bar{\psi}(V_\text{base})\]

    A positive \(\Delta_b\) means corrupting block \(b\) makes the generated video look less plausible to the probe.

Results

What the hidden states know

Key result

Intuitive physics is not unique to V-JEPA-style encoders: video diffusion models have it too

The evidence so far for intuitive physics in video models comes from self-supervised encoders such as V-JEPA, trained to predict masked parts of a video in representation space. Video diffusion models are trained for something else entirely: generating video. Yet linear probes on their hidden states separate plausible from implausible clips as well as, and often better than, V-JEPA 2 and VideoMAE, including their largest ViT-g and Giant versions.

On IntPhys, all three diffusion models score above every encoder; on InfLevel, the two families are on par. Physical structure is not the product of one training objective. It also emerges from learning to generate.

The signal is not in the input

Probing the VAE latents directly, before any transformer computation, stays close to chance, and under t-SNE plausible and implausible clips are intermixed everywhere. V-JEPA's physical signal comes from an embedding space trained to predict. Here, nothing in the input or the objective asks for it: the structure is built by the denoiser while it transports noise to video.

Where it emerges

Physics emerges at intermediate depth, across the whole trajectory

Probing every block at every noise level, the best block scores above 76% at every noise level except the clean endpoint, for all three models. WAN-1.3B's richest block reaches 88.9%. The signal is localized in depth, not in time: the best blocks cluster in the middle third of the network, the same intermediate-depth emergence reported for V-JEPA.

What drives it

Early blocks inject the physics; middle blocks expose it

Corrupting one block at a time while generating plausible IntPhys scenes shows which blocks the probe-assessed plausibility depends on. The effect is spread across depth but structured: early blocks dominate for WAN-1.3B and CogVideoX-2B, while LTX-2B is bimodal, with early and late blocks both sensitive. The most decodable blocks are not the most causal ones. Physical structure appears to be set up early and propagated through the network, becoming linearly readable only at intermediate depth.

Beyond plausibility

Beyond plausibility: the initial conditions are in there too

On PhyWorld parabolas, a linear regressor on a single block recovers the ball's initial position with R² = 1.00 for every model, and its initial velocity with R² between 0.88 (WAN-1.3B) and 0.99, on par with V-JEPA 2 and VideoMAE-Large. The hidden states carry not just that a trajectory obeys gravity, but the specific numbers that generated it. Neither is supervised during diffusion training.

Model capacity

Narrower transformers give a cleaner readout

Best per-video accuracy falls as the DiT's hidden width grows, from 1536 (WAN) to 1920 (CogVideoX) to 2048 (LTX). The smallest and most compressed model reads out physics best.

One reading is capacity. A narrow model must spend its width on the coarse spatiotemporal structure needed for coherent denoising, while a wide one also spends it on texture, diluting the physical signal. The same trade-off fits the regression result above, where WAN-1.3B is the weakest at the fine-grained velocity. We treat the trend as suggestive: the three models differ in more than width.

The trajectory matters

Physics lives in the flow, not at its endpoints

Inverting with 20 steps instead of 100 still recovers noise that regenerates a faithful video, yet probe accuracy collapses from 0.82 to 0.57, close to chance. A more accurate integrator at the same 20 steps recovers part of it. Decodability tracks how closely the discrete path follows the model's continuous ODE: a coarse trajectory lands in the right place, but through states the model would never visit.
